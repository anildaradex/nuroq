# Shift the score's weight toward trend

The plan's skeleton holds up: deterministic rules first, a market regime gate, a bounded LLM, and a notify-only rollout are each consistent with the best evidence found. Three parts need to change before it is built. First, about 65 of the 100 score points reward the shape of the pullback (depth, moving-average "support", reversal confirmation), where evidence is mixed to absent and the edge fades within days, while the best-evidenced factors (6- to 12-month relative strength and nearness to the 52-week high) get 20 points and zero points respectively. Second, the stop rule takes the *tighter* of two levels with a 1 ATR floor, and every test of dip-buying found says tight stops destroy that kind of entry; it should take the wider, with a 1.5 ATR floor. Third, the promotion bar of 8 weeks and 60 picks cannot detect a realistic edge: by the researchers' power arithmetic it passes a zero-edge strategy roughly 20% of the time, so it should be recast as a plumbing-and-consistency gate, with evidence of edge coming from an 8–10 year survivorship-free backtest. No published LLM trading agent uses its model the way this plan does; they let the model decide direction and size, and the one long, bias-controlled study finds they underperform buy-and-hold, which supports keeping the AI small but says nothing about whether 10 points and a veto help. No study tests this combined rule set on US single stocks after 2015 with realistic costs, so most recommended numbers below are starting values and are marked as judgment where they are.

## What to buy: keep the gate and filters, rebalance the score

### A buffered 200-day gate cuts drawdown, not whipsaw-free return

Keep the SPY regime gate, but do not expect it to raise return. Trading the raw S&P 500 close against its 200-day average since 2000 returned **3.87% a year against 4.54% for buy-and-hold**, with 173 crosses, 12 of them in 2022 alone; smoothing the close with a 5-day average cut the crosses since 2020 from 22 to 7 and beat buy-and-hold with lower drawdown, and a 1% band improved it further ([StockCharts](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-arthurhill-2022-12-sp-500-crosses-above-200day-sm-360/)). The most direct evidence for a pullback strategy is an ETF study in which a 200-day filter in front of a buy-the-dip rule gave the best minimum Sharpe of five filter lengths ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/testing-an-ai-assisted-research-workflow-for-multi-asset-pullback-strategy-discovery/)). The academic support is that momentum profits are significantly higher after up markets ([Cooper, Gutierrez and Hameed](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/361185)) and that momentum crashes occur in panic states after market declines ([Daniel and Moskowitz](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20439)). The gate has a cost the plan should state: returns to buying short-term losers rise sharply when VIX is high ([Nagel](https://www.nber.org/papers/w17653)), so the gate switches dip-buying off exactly when its premium is largest. For a long-only book with stops, the drawdown benefit wins. The plan's validation table should also be reworded: the StockCharts source shows the raw cross returning less than buy-and-hold, and the researchers could not confirm the 57%-to-21% drawdown figures quoted there.

### Eligibility rules are sound; the 25% threshold is convention

The $5 floor is the standard academic liquidity screen ([Jegadeesh and Titman 2001](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/218/Momentum.pdf)), and the reason is that momentum's large paper returns sit in the stocks that cost most to trade ([Lesmond, Schill and Zhou](https://www.bauer.uh.edu/rsusmel/phd/Lesmond_et%20al%20_2004_JFE.pdf)). Nearness to the 52-week high predicts returns better than past returns do ([George and Hwang, via Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/the-secret-to-momentum-is-the-52-week-high/)), and the distance between short and long moving averages earns about 9% annualized alpha, stronger on the long side ([Avramov, Kaplanski and Subrahmanyam](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wly:revfec:v:39:y:2021:i:2:p:127-145)). The exact template, however, is a screen description from community summaries of Minervini, not a backtest ([ChartMill](https://www.chartmill.com/documentation/stock-screener/technical-analysis-trading-strategies/496-Mark-Minervini-Trend-Template-A-Step-by-Step-Guide-for-Beginners)), and no study validates 25% against alternatives. The earnings rule has a hole: it blocks entry within 5 days of a report, but the time stop is 10 days, so a position entered 6–10 days before earnings is carried through it. The exclusion gives up a documented premium of over 60 bps a month ([Frazzini and Lamont, NBER Digest](https://www.nber.org/digest/mar08/stocks-rise-around-earnings-announcements)), which is an acceptable price for a book whose 1% risk sizing is void across an earnings gap.

### Sixty-five points currently reward the weakest evidence

The pullback leg is real but small. Among the largest US stocks, reversal earns 30–50 bps a week net of costs ([de Groot, Huij and Zhou](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jbfina:v:36:y:2012:i:2:p:371-382)), and in the ETF study the Sharpe ratio fell **from 0.95 at a 1-day hold to 0.54 at 7 days** ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/testing-an-ai-assisted-research-workflow-for-multi-asset-pullback-strategy-discovery/)). Index-level dip-buying above the 200-day average returned +1.7% a year against +13.5% for buy-and-hold over 2016–2026, with average loss (2.10%) larger than average win (1.22%) ([Backtrex](https://backtrex.com/en/backtests/connors-rsi-2-sp-500)). A 10-day hold therefore has to be paid for mostly by the trend leg. Against that, the score's second-largest component, proximity to the 20- or 50-day average, has no supporting evidence at all: the only rigorous support-level study concerns intraday FX levels published by dealers ([Osler, NY Fed](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/epr/00v06n2/0007osle.html)), and the component largely restates pullback depth. Reversal confirmation is ambiguous: waiting for a bounce left results "virtually unchanged" for a fast signal and helped a slower one ([StockCharts](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-arthurhill-2016-07-systemtrader---testing-a-basic-mean-reversion-system---catch-the-knife-or-wait-for-the-bounce-/)); it shapes risk and is not a source of return.

Relative strength is the best-supported component and the worst-specified. Momentum is significant for 3- to 12-month formation periods ([Springer review](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s11408-022-00417-8)), but the literature centres on 6–12 months skipping the latest month, and for a long-only investor 12-2 and 12-7 momentum perform similarly ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/when-academics-disagree-on-momentum-investing/)). A 3-month window ending today contains the pullback itself, so the component mechanically penalises the stocks the pullback term rewards. The volume rule points the right way: one-month losers in the lowest-turnover decile reverse (−1.41% a month winners-minus-losers) while those in the highest-turnover decile continue (+1.37%) ([Medhat and Schmeling, via Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/short-term-momentum/)).

## How to trade it: 09:35 holds up, the stop and order type do not

### Entry timing is defensible and the open itself should be avoided

Peer-reviewed work finds that momentum profits accrue overnight and that their intraday component is insignificant ([Lou, Polk and Skouras](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/87481)). This is the strongest structural finding against an open-bell design: the first overnight leg after the signal is forfeited and the entry day's session is expected to add nothing. A multi-day hold collects later overnights, so the cost is one-off, and it has not been measured for this strategy. The case for 09:35 rather than the opening print is firmer. In high-attention stocks, overnight returns of about +10 bp are followed by trading-day reversals of about −7 bp, and the implicit cost of buying at the open frequently exceeds the effective half-spread ([Berkman et al.](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/paying-attention-overnight-returns-and-the-hidden-cost-of-buying-at-the-open/F9AAD159B512C651F09D5D52011D88E0)); sub-hour reversals reflect temporary liquidity imbalances ([Heston, Korajczyk and Sadka](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2010/intraday-patterns-in-the-cross-section-of-stock-returns)). Nothing found distinguishes 09:35 from 10:00. Since Notify mode places no orders, logging several reference prices per pick answers the question for free.

Two data facts change what "the 09:35 price" should mean. Alpaca's free real-time feed is IEX only, with SIP data free once 15 minutes old ([Alpaca market data FAQ](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/market-data-faq)), and Alpaca staff put IEX at about 2–3% of trades ([Alpaca forum](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/difference-between-iex-and-sip-in-historical-data/10191)); a last-trade price for a mid-cap can be stale. Gap statistics are practitioner-only and index-based: SPY gaps of 0.10–0.25% fill the same day 77.0% of the time against 41.7% for gaps of 0.5–1% ([edgeful](https://www.edgeful.com/blog/posts/gap-fill-report)). These should not be read as single-stock probabilities, and the case for skipping large gap-ups rests on reward-to-risk geometry.

The order mechanics matter once Approve or Auto exists. Bracket exit legs are inactive until the entry is completely filled, work only in regular hours, and a stop becomes a market order with no price guarantee ([Alpaca orders documentation](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/orders-at-alpaca)). Paper trading gives random partial fills about 10% of the time and does not model market impact, latency slippage or queue position ([Alpaca paper trading](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/paper-trading)). The executor therefore needs an explicit handler for a partly filled entry with no active stop, an app-side job for the 10-day time stop (a bracket has no time leg), and reconciliation after every exit because both legs can fill in a fast market.

### The stop is the weakest-supported rule in the plan

Stops reduce risk; they do not add return unless losses tend to continue. Under a random walk a stop always lowers expected return ([Kaminski and Lo](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/114876/Lo_When%20Do%20Stop-Loss.pdf)), and on 25,997 US stocks trailing stops lowered both return and tail risk, with the risk benefit disappearing for stops tighter than 10% once costs are included ([Dai et al., via CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/trailing-stop-loss-effectiveness-for-stocks)). For dip-buying specifically, one S&P 500 backtest over 2000–2026 found that adding a 5% stop to the same entries cut the annual return **from 8.22% to 1.05%** and deepened maximum drawdown from −18.63% to −41.78% ([SetupAlpha](https://setup4alpha.substack.com/p/stop-loss-mean-reversion-backtest)). This is a single-author result, but it agrees with every other practitioner test found. A stop is still worth having in an unattended account, as a disaster stop that bounds single-name damage and gives the 1% sizing a denominator.

The fixed 3 ATR target is supported by neither body of evidence. Dip-buying systems exit into strength, typically a close above the 5-day average, with no fixed target ([StockCharts ChartSchool](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/trading-strategies-and-models/trading-strategies/rsi-2)); a trend thesis would not cap winners. The 10-day time stop sits inside the one-week to one-month window where short-term reversal is documented ([Alpha Architect review](https://alphaarchitect.com/quantitative-momentum-research-short-term-return-reversal/)). On sizing, 1% risk divided by an ATR stop is already equal-volatility sizing, and the academic return claim for a separate volatility-targeting layer failed out of sample ([Cederburg et al., via Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/does-portfolio-timing-based-on-volatility-signals-outperform-buy-and-hold/)). What the plan lacks is a cap on position notional: the researchers' arithmetic shows a 1 ATR stop on a typical stock produces a position near 40% of the budget, so a 10% overnight gap costs four times the intended risk. The sector cap is the best-supported portfolio limit, because industry momentum explains much of stock momentum ([Moskowitz and Grinblatt](https://ideas.repec.org/p/wop/chispw/352.html)) and a relative-strength screen will keep surfacing the same industries.

## The LLM: a bounded role no benchmark has tested

Published agents put the model in charge. TradingAgents uses analysts, bull and bear researchers, a trader and a risk team, and was evaluated over three months, with the authors noting that its best Sharpe "exceeds our expected empirical range" ([TradingAgents](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2412.20138)). In StockBench the model sets direction and dollar targets ([StockBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02209v2)); in LiveTradeBench it outputs portfolio weights ([LiveTradeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03628v1)). None uses an LLM as a small add-on to deterministic rules, so their results are evidence about "the LLM decides", not about this design.

That evidence is poor for the agents. FINSABER tested FinMem and FinAgent over 2004–2024 on survivorship-controlled data with commissions and found no significant alpha (all p > 0.34); on a momentum-selected universe buy-and-hold's Sharpe was **0.384 against 0.025 and 0.104**, and the agents were too timid in bull markets and too aggressive in bear markets ([FINSABER](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078v4)). Live benchmarks run 24 to 82 trading days and mostly ignore costs. In StockBench, 8 of 14 models beat equal-weight buy-and-hold's +0.4% return, all by 2.1 points or less with no costs modelled, and rankings reshuffled between windows. The plan's validation table says most agents "fail to beat" the baseline, which matches the paper's abstract but overstates its results table; a more accurate wording is "finish within about 2 points of equal-weight buy-and-hold over 82 days before costs". Model strength does not predict results: the correlation between LMArena score and stock return was 0.054 ([LiveTradeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03628v1)), and agent frameworks drive outcomes more than model backbones ([Agent Market Arena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11695)). Backtested agent returns decay 50–72% once training-data leakage is removed ([Profit Mirage](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07920v1)), a figure the researchers took from a search snippet and did not check against the paper's text.

Where LLMs measurably help is next-day news classification, which is a different trade. The headline-sentiment effect is concentrated in small caps and bad news, is feasible only at very low trading costs, and declines as adoption rises ([Lopez-Lira and Tang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07619v6)). The closest analogue to the plan's grounded path found that a search-enabled LLM's top-rated Russell 1000 names earned alpha while its bottom-rated names were indistinguishable from the market ([Chen and Pu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11958)). This is one unreplicated preprint, and it implies a generic "unattractive" veto carries no information; a veto has to be tied to a specific, verifiable event. Numeric scores are also less stable than labels: a case study found significant inconsistencies between an LLM's numeric sentiment score and its own categorical label ([arXiv 2504.04462](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.04462v1)), while aggregated classifications stay stable ([arXiv 2503.16974](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.16974v1)). Grounding adds a manipulation surface: in simulation, homoglyph edits broke stock-name mapping 99% of the time and hidden HTML text flipped sentiment 67% of the time ([Rizvani et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.13082)).

## Proving it: sixty picks test the plumbing, not the edge

The following figures are the researchers' own arithmetic on a standard one-sample power formula and assumed inputs; they are not sourced results. Detecting a 0.2–0.5% per-trade edge against 4–6% per-trade noise needs roughly **500 to 7,000 independent trades**. Sixty picks at 5% noise give a confidence interval of ±1.27% and about 12% power for a 0.5% edge. Overlapping 5-day windows and same-day picks shrink the effective sample further, plausibly to 20–36. Under zero true edge, "BUY beats SPY after costs" passes about 40% of the time and "BUY beats WATCH" about 50%, so both together promote a skill-less strategy about one time in five. The cited literature supports the direction: same-date clustering causes serious over-rejection in event-study tests ([Kolari and Pynnönen](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v23y2010i11p3996-4025.html)), a new strategy needs a t-ratio above 3.0 once multiple testing is counted ([Harvey, Liu and Zhu](https://nber.org/papers/w20592)), and only live data and fresh history are truly out of sample ([Arnott, Harvey and Markowitz, via Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/2018/12/protocol-to-prevent-quants-gone-wild)).

The backtest plan needs the larger correction. With five years of daily data, about 45 strategy variations are enough for the best to show a Sharpe of 1.0 by chance ([Bailey et al.](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/overfit-tools-at.pdf)); the researchers' extension of that result puts the budget at roughly 3–7 variations on 1–2 years of data. Tuning the plan's ten or so starting values on a short window guarantees overfitting. Survivorship is the dominant data risk for a long-only trend strategy: yfinance mostly returns nothing for delisted tickers ([MarketParquet](https://marketparquet.com/guides/yfinance-limitations-backtesting)), and in one research report substituting a point-in-time universe removed the apparent edge entirely ([equity signal research report](https://nnicholas-c.github.io/Return-prediction-signal/equity_signal_research_report.pdf)). Earnings dates must be those known on the decision day, or the earnings filter looks better in backtest than it can be live ([E.P. Chan](https://epchan.blogspot.com/2016)).

The 10 bps per side assumption is conservative rather than optimistic: S&P 500 spread costs run just over 4.5 bps, with the largest names near 1 bp ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/sampling-sp-500-minimize-spreads)). The larger point is proportion. A 20 bps round trip is 40–100% of a 0.2–0.5% gross edge, so the cost should be measured from logged quotes instead of assumed.

## What the research could not establish, and which numbers are judgment

No study was found that tests the combined rule set (regime gate, trend template, pullback, confirmation) on US single stocks with post-2015 data and realistic costs; the plan's own harness will be the first such evidence. The specific gaps that bear on decisions are these. No source tests ATR-multiple stops or fixed ATR targets on pullback entries in individual stocks. No source compares market-on-open, 09:35, 10:00 and close entries for multi-day holds, or gives single-stock gap behaviour by gap size in ATR units. No recent minute-by-minute spread data for the opening period was found, so "spreads normalise by 09:35" is plausible but unquantified. No study measures the value of an LLM event veto on a momentum or pullback book, no calibration study exists for 0–10 conviction scores, and no multi-year live evaluation of any LLM agent on US equities was found. Whether the regime gate adds value cannot be validated in any forward window of practical length. Whether Alpaca brackets accept fractional quantities, and how a partly filled bracket entry is protected, were not confirmed from documentation. The project's own data subscription and feed were not inspected.

Several sources were read only as abstracts, secondary summaries or search snippets, including Lou, Polk and Skouras, Berkman et al., Faber, Han et al., Profit Mirage and the Alpha Arena coverage. Practitioner backtests (StockCharts, Quantpedia, Backtrex, SetupAlpha) share the usual weaknesses of little or no cost modelling and in-sample parameter choice, and should be read as directional.

The following recommended numbers are judgment calls, not sourced values: the ±1% regime band as applied to an entries-only gate; the $20M dollar-volume floor and its $10M fallback; the 15% and 20% alternatives for the 52-week-high gate; every proposed score weight; the 1.5–4 ATR pullback band; the 2× volume cut-off; the 1.5 ATR stop floor and 3 ATR cap; the 3 ATR target; the 20–25% notional cap; 2 entries per day and 2 per sector; the 1.0 and 0.5 ATR gap thresholds, the gap-down skip, the 0.30% and 0.10 ATR spread limits, the 15-second quote age and the 2-minute cancel; the flag-to-points mapping, the 2-of-3 agreement rule and the 10-day recency window; and, in the promotion ladder, 12 weeks and 40 BUY picks, the 70% plateau rule, the size steps, the slippage bands and the −6R and −10R triggers. The power, design-effect and trial-budget figures are the researchers' calculations on standard formulas with assumed inputs (5% per-trade noise, pairwise correlation of 0.1–0.3), to be replaced by values measured in the backtest.

## Conclusion

The research changes what the strategy is understood to be. The plan describes a pullback strategy filtered by trend; the evidence supports a trend strategy that uses a pullback to time entry and shape risk. That reading resolves most of the individual findings at once: weight the score toward relative strength and nearness to the high, widen the stop so it fires only when the trend thesis breaks, and stop expecting the dip itself to pay for a 10-day hold.

It also changes what the notify phase is for. Eight to twelve weeks cannot show an edge, but it can measure things no paper supplies: the real spread at 09:35, the cost of forfeiting the first overnight leg, whether confirmation and the gap skips help, and whether vetoed names do worse than kept ones. Each of those needs a field in the log from day one, because none can be reconstructed afterwards. The backtest harness, moved ahead of the notify phase and run on survivorship-free history with a small logged trial budget, is where a claim of edge has to come from.
